The saying “Lottery is a tax on stupidity” is well known.

Since the expected return on a lottery ticket is usually less than 500 won, buying one means entering a game that loses money.

People apply strict standards to money and economics like this and want to make the best choice.

By contrast, people generally take a complacent attitude toward their health.

They justify harming their health by citing people who enjoyed alcohol, cigarettes, and fast food yet lived long, healthy lives.

Although they know very few people have genes equivalent to winning the lottery, most act as though their own genetic lottery win were a foregone conclusion.

Some argue that individual effort does not matter because genetics has a greater influence on health than any other factor.

Others say we have only one life, and should enjoy what we want and live a short but full one.

But there can never be success or satisfaction in a life that puts health on the back burner.

A strong mind comes from a healthy body, and it is clear that higher thought and wisdom come from physical activity.

Doing our best in our given circumstances and making health our highest priority until we die is how we protect our lives.

If we abuse the health we borrow from youth, everything built upon that health we took for granted can collapse in an instant.

Accounts of the regrets of great figures who insisted on present achievement at the expense of future health demonstrate this.

> If you do not make time for your health, you will have to make time for illness.
>
> Robin Sharma

The pleasure and pain humans can feel are limited.

Through the dopamine system, sustained stimulation or sustained pain eventually becomes a new normal.

This suggests that a rich-get-richer, poor-get-poorer pattern also exists in how we receive dopamine.

People who continuously receive quick dopamine passively will pursue those stimuli and feel anxious in the stable state of everyday life,

while those who block quick dopamine and consistently receive slower, effort-based rewards will more actively pursue achievements that take a long time.

For the latter, a virtuous cycle emerges: present discomfort prevents greater future discomfort, and they feel at ease in situations others find uncomfortable.

Satisfaction from challenges and achievements must be entirely about myself rather than comparison with others,

and ultimately, I should be able to enjoy the supreme pleasures offered by nature and everyday life.

> Rest and travel based on comparison and showing off
>
> merely change the form of the activity from earning money to spending it,
>
> leaving only depleted foundations and physical and mental fatigue that has not recovered.
