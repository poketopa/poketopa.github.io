## 🎊 Passed the first round for Woowacourse cohort 8

After the long precourse, I was fortunate enough to **pass the first screening** and start preparing for the **coding test**!

Results were scheduled for **3 p.m.**, but emails arrived at wildly different times for different people.
(Some received theirs several hours later.)

Mine arrived at **3:02**, and it was a truly nerve-racking wait…

# 1. Preparation
## 1) Solving past problems
From the day I received the first-round acceptance email, I had **about twelve days** until the coding test.

Unlike conventional problem-solving questions, where you identify the required data structure and solve the problem quickly and efficiently,

Woowacourse's test requires completing an object-oriented program according to the requirements. So I felt I needed my own strategy and the routine most comfortable for me.

Also, because each problem takes considerable time, I set a goal of **focusing on one problem a day**.

## 2) Finding what I lacked

### Addressing my weaknesses
I thought it would be hard to improve my algorithm-solving ability itself in around ten days.

Finding and addressing my weaknesses seemed the top priority, so rather than joining a study group, I worked on problems alone and tried to identify what I lacked.

By solving past problems, I wanted to identify the **issues** that frequently arose for me and prepare **countermeasures**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-최종-코딩테스트-후기-03.webp" width="60%">
</p>

Since I was solving algorithm problems every day, I was confident about coding tests!

Or so… I thought. But after solving a few problems, I could not handle **errors arising in the test code**.

If I failed to resolve test-code errors during the real test, I could receive **zero points**, so I tried to focus on understanding the errors that might occur.

I also ran into errors several times because methods in Woowacourse's `camp.nextstep.edu.missionutils` library returned `ImmutableCollection`, so I checked how to work with the methods the library provided.

### Templates
Many posts I read before the test advised figuring out what you would need and when, then preparing **templates**.

But personally, I was not used to deciding in advance when, where, and how I would think and act, so I tried to focus on reading the problem carefully and following my **train of thought**.

- Read the problem carefully.
- When stuck, commit first and try running it.
- Always update the README when committing.

I kept only the **most important routines** like these in mind for the test, and briefly organized possible issues and useful syntax in **Notion**.

**The cohort 8 final test differed considerably from previous tests.**

- Four hours for solving the problem + one hour for a reflection essay (no pushes after 5 p.m.).
- A variation on the week 3 mission.
- An added challenge assignment.

Since it was **a variation of the Lotto problem** from week 3, implementing it within the time limit did not seem difficult.

What mattered, though, was finishing quickly and then **deciding what feature to add for the challenge assignment**.

There was a rule against changing the basic methods provided in `OutputView` and `InputView`, so I could not change the inputs, and it seemed the challenge allowed only additional internal logic and output.

## Challenge assignment

Implementing the features and checking that the test code built took about three hours, leaving only about one hour to add the challenge. I wanted to connect **what I had felt** while writing my **Woowacourse application** and doing the **precourse** with my **experience** of the **week 3 Lotto mission**.

### My story

After submitting the open mission, while waiting for the first screening result, I read a book called `The Slow-Aging Mindset`. This sentence stayed with me most.

> **People call those who buy lottery tickets hoping to win foolish, yet eat and behave as though they themselves have won the genetic lottery for good health. **

The point was that health does not suddenly improve or deteriorate through one specific action; we need to manage it consistently every day and benefit from its compound interest.

Reading this made me wonder whether I had treated **my growth and success** like **a lottery**, too. Honestly, while writing my application, I vaguely thought that getting into Woowacourse would make me **a good coder** and provide **a royal road to employment at a good company**.

But studying during the precourse felt very different from that **royal road**. Although all we were given was **surprisingly simple assignments and requirements**, solving the same problems alongside many people was enough to help me learn and grow a lot. I felt that what I needed was to keep holding on to **small efforts and achievements** and **grow consistently**. My growth throughout life could not be a lottery-like windfall; it had to come from **small daily efforts**.

In that sense, I thought effort and growth also had **compound interest**. Compound interest grows frighteningly large over time, and today's small efforts could produce **enormous results** decades later. I might not be different tomorrow from who I am today, but by consistently accumulating **the compound interest of effort**, perhaps I could become someone who easily achieves **challenges and accomplishments** that now feel overwhelming, someone impressive who can do great things without difficulty.

When implementing the **Lotto mission** in week 3, I ran the program countless times but never saw winnings exceed the purchase price for **a return over 100%**. But could **a modest beginning** and **consistent effort** produce **lottery-winning levels of growth**? That thought led me to create **a compound-interest calculator**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-최종-코딩테스트-후기-14.webp" width="60%">

We had to describe the challenge in README.md, so I wrote what is shown in the image and added a feature list.

Honestly, rereading it on the train home, I felt a little embarrassed, wondering whether I had gotten too carried away and written something cringeworthy.

<p align="center">
  <img src="/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-최종-코딩테스트-후기-15.webp" width="80%">
</p>

Using the required `Randoms.pickUniqueNumbersInRange` method, I assumed **random annual growth of 0–30%**.

The starting value is the amount spent on lottery tickets, and the program prints how much it grows **every ten years**, through **seventy years** and beyond.

<p align="center">
  <img src="/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-최종-코딩테스트-후기-16.webp" width="60%">
</p>

This is the result for buying **100,000 won** of lottery tickets.

Running the code at the venue produced such an absurdly large value that it made me laugh while reminding me how powerful compound interest really is.

I considered using `BigInteger` instead of `long`, but did not have time to change it.

# 4. Closing thoughts

**Between finishing the precourse and taking the coding test, I thought through many personal concerns.**

**Having finished the second semester of my fourth year, under pressure to prepare to enter society, I wondered how I should live to find happiness.**

**I do not dispute that happiness is life's ultimate purpose. For me, growth brings satisfaction and happiness throughout life.**

**But growth brings endless challenges, and challenges inevitably come with trials and hardship.**

**In that sense, I think I need an attitude of humbly accepting every process and outcome within a challenge and continuing forward.**

**Yet I still interpret those trials and hardships as personal failures, so the truth is that they frighten me.**

**I also worry whether I can be happy through a process filled with such difficulties.**

**I originally did not intend to write this coding test retrospective. **

**A retrospective written after seeing the final result in about ten days could be a definite success story.**

**Even so, I wrote this beforehand because I wanted to see the inevitable trials and hardships of the life ahead simply as part of my growth, rather than as defeat or failure.**

**I am still very afraid of future failures, but beginning with this post, I want to love the process of my growth.**

**I will end with the thought that there may be trials, but there is no failure. Thank you!**
