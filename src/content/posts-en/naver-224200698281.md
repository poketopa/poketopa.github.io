Why does effort let us down?

## 1. Simply putting in time is meaningless.

Deliberate practice is necessary. Repeated deliberate practice can make an ability unconscious, and from there, repeating new deliberate practice can bring new abilities into the unconscious realm. By repeating this process, anyone can master any field.

## 2. You need a good environment and a good mentor.

Deliberate practice requires criteria that let you check whether you have achieved your goals, and a mentor who can assess you against those criteria.

---

These two points are all the book says, but the flood of peripheral explanations throughout made it tiring.

The book says that people's abilities are not genetically determined and that anyone can attain mastery in a particular field through effort, but I disagree.

For someone who is "able to" make that effort, I do of course think anyone can reach a certain level in a particular field, even if their pace differs according to their genetic traits.

But I think having the ability or environment to devote yourself entirely to a particular field is determined wholly by your surroundings, genetics, and experiences.

Because I think having the opportunity or ability to immerse yourself in something is beyond your control, regardless of what you want, it feels almost like fate to me.

The book's statements implying that '*You must not say, "I was not born talented, so trying will not help."*' actually felt disingenuous to me.
