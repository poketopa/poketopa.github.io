> My aunt looked like a girl who had opened a strange, empty novel with a title but no text
>
> and was left utterly bewildered.
>
> Contradictions — Yang Gui-ja

Why did that passage strike me so deeply?

In the novel, the aunt says that wherever they go, her husband is done after taking just three pictures.

***"******One of me, one of him, and one of the two of us. Then we come back to the hotel at night, he adds up what we spent that day and what we expect to spend tomorrow, sorts out his wallet, and goes straight to sleep. He has no idea that I spend the whole night pacing by the window, emptying the bottles of alcohol in the fridge."***

The aunt said she had lived in a peace like the inside of a grave.

It suddenly occurred to me that this emptiness was like social media.

A photo, an emoji, a few comments.

Trips for the sake of posting.

Photos for the sake of showing off.

A life for the sake of display.

Such fleeting things are so deeply entrenched that sometimes they feel like the natural order of things.

No matter how often I hear and take to heart

the emptiness of a life like a mannequin in a shop window,

the insecurity of a life like a castle built on sand,

and the futility of a life like hollow fireworks,

it seems I cannot help feeling envy and impatience about them.

I think the way to make life more colorful is to recognize what I have and the beauty around me, remember that happiness and satisfaction drawn from myself rather than others enrich my life,

and keep discovering the value of those things, one by one, for as long as I live.

In an interview with Han Kang, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature, I read that she had cried while listening to an AKMU song in a taxi.

How often have I listened to a song, reflected on its lyrics, and connected with them?

How much of that beauty around me am I missing as I go through life?

How different is the depth of emotion and experience between someone who does that and someone who does not?

Recognizing the beauty of what I love and of the things around me, and filling my life little by little.

I think that is one of life's most important tasks.
