## Precourse week 2 retrospective

Week 2 overlapped with midterms, so I was incredibly busy!!

Studying for exams, doing the precourse assignment, attending study sessions, and trying to swim every day left me short on time.

Still, I tried hard to study what I needed to learn.

## General / backend feedback

![](/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-프리코스-2주차-회고-01.webp)

After the first week's mission, we received general and backend feedback. The image shows part of it.

I saw the weekly feedback as a standard of **“If you're doing the precourse, you should know at least this much,”** so I tried to study and understand everything in the general and backend feedback.

I thought knowing exactly where I was lacking was important if I wanted to learn as much as possible over the five-week precourse.

So in week 2, I spent more time reviewing feedback, studying new concepts, and trying to design systematically than actually writing code.

## PR code reviews

After the first week's mission, Discord was full of requests for PR reviews.

I joined in, hoping to find my own shortcomings and learn from other people's knowledge.

One thing I felt clearly in the process was that _**understanding someone else's code was much harder than writing my own.**_

I understood why readability and maintainability are overwhelmingly prioritized over performance.

> Coding is collaboration, after all…

The code I saw in PR reviews was distinctly different from mine.

Packages were clearly separated by roles and responsibilities—domain, validation, exceptions—and the code was easy to read.

Unsure which development methodologies I should learn and incorporate, or how to apply them to the assignment, I asked someone who seemed experienced in the PR reviews for a review and guidance.

![](/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-프리코스-2주차-회고-02.webp)

Thankfully, they explained how I could apply unfamiliar concepts such as the MVC pattern, layered architecture, and principles for separating methods to the assignment.

Concepts I needed to learn poured down like a waterfall, and a fundamental question kept circling in my mind.

**_Would applying such a large design to a simple application be overengineering?_**

I found an answer to that concern in the requirements:

> “Limit indentation depth to 2 and separate methods.”

I wondered whether what the precourse wanted was more than **simply implementing functionality**:

**even if it feels excessive now, try various architectural patterns, refactor, review with others, and gain as much experience as possible.**

## Wrapping up week 2

After submitting, I compared my week 1 and week 2 code and felt I had made considerable progress structurally.

What pleased me was **being able to explain why I had designed the improved structure that way, just as I had resolved to do in week 1.**

Of course, there were regrets. Studying and applying a new architecture left me without enough time to learn unit testing.

I wanted to prove that the architecture I had designed really was safe. I plan to study test code more in week 3.

I think week 2 brought even more growth than week 1.

Thinking about how much more I might grow as the difficulty increases in week 3, I feel more anticipation than fear.
