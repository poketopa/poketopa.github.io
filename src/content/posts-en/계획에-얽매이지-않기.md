My personal learning goal for March was to read a book.

I thought its difficulty was manageable. But the Blackjack review dragged on, and I spent time on it right up to the day before the Janggi mission, so I never got to read it.

I did not read the book, but I learned how to explain my thinking and the reasons for my choices to a reviewer.

Should I consider that time a failure because I did not stick to my plan?

Finishing a plan makes me feel I have studied well. Conversely, when something remains unfinished, I feel I have not done things properly, even if I have done a lot of other work.

In April, I thought goals like that could actually constrain me. Instead of setting an amount to cover, I made it my goal to keep studying without giving up and complete the mission.

In June, after hearing a coach's advice and a talk by a working professional, I changed my study plan again.

As AI came up more and more, I started questioning whether the studying I had planned was really what I needed now. I set it aside and chose a different challenge.

I thought the first plan I made could not always remain the best choice. Trying things myself and changing direction also helped me learn what I liked and how I learned.

## But not letting it become an excuse

Yet if I say I learned something from anything, does that make every unfinished task okay?

I could use the same words to describe changing direction after discovering something more important and giving up because something was hard.

When an assignment asked about the difference between positive thinking and rationalization, I answered this way.

> Does that thought lead to better action?

Even though I did not read the book, I learned something. But that does not mean I should rationalize underestimating the time the mission would take or failing to set aside time for reading.

I do not want to judge learning solely by whether I finished my plan. But I do not want to use "I learned something" to cover up why I failed to finish, either.

I think I can only convince myself if I can explain what I learned and what I can therefore do differently next time.
