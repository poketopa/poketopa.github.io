This was something Ludovico said during our discussion of The Stranger.

> Is sadness sad?

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

The question was whether sadness is sad in itself.

It may sound like wordplay, but to me it was a topic well worth thinking about.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

We feel sad while reading books or watching films, and sometimes we cry.

The emotion we feel in that process is clearly "sadness," yet we do not remember the time spent feeling sad as merely sad.

Instead, I think we regard it as a valuable experience.

That made me wonder whether there might be two kinds of emotion.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

The first is the emotion we feel in our hearts.

It is an emotion like the unmistakable sadness we feel when something unfortunate happens, when we fail to reach a goal, when we read a sad book, or when we drift away from someone dear to us.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

The second is the emotion with which we close a memory.

If the first arises in the heart, the second is brought to a close in the mind.

It is looking back on an experience and remembering it as "happy" or "sad."

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Emotions felt in the heart are difficult to handle because they are already arising before we have even defined them.

But the emotion with which I close a memory seems able to change depending on how I view those feelings and memories.

If I value even the moments of sadness and difficult times, I can bring them to a close as good memories.

Conversely, if I cannot view even happy and grateful moments positively, they remain merely ordinary times.

If bringing my past to a close is entirely up to me, I want to close those times with feelings that they were valuable and enjoyable.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Right now, I am running without pause at Woowa Tech Course. Every day feels overwhelming.

It is hard. Unmistakably hard. It has been a long time since I rested with an easy mind.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Even now, writing this, I am struggling, but I still want to try to enjoy the process.

With the belief that this time will make my future self shine brighter.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Even if it does not lead to the results I hoped for, I want to be someone who can recognize the value of the effort and growth along the way.

Then even this difficult time will remain a meaningful part of the process for me.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

I hope everything about me has meaning. I still believe that now.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

> <em><strong>"There are only two ways to live your life. One is as though nothing is a miracle. The other is as though everything is a miracle."</strong></em><br /><br />Albert Einstein
