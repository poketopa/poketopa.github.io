## Is knowledge power, or is ignorance bliss?

Does intelligence determine human value?

I dare say it does.

How can we answer the question of what makes humans different from other animals?

## The immediate answer would probably be 'We are intelligent.'

Personally, I think the human privilege may be to step outside our assigned genetic obligations (even though escaping them completely is impossible) and witness, create, and feel values beyond reproduction and survival.

## Only humans can answer 'Why were you born?' with something other than 'To perpetuate our genes.'

Because I think intelligence determines the depth of thought with which we struggle against those instincts, intelligence can, to some extent, be called a core human value.

Then do highly intelligent people enjoy greater happiness and pleasure than others because they can find and feel more kinds of value?

Not at all.

Existential freedom instead becomes a shackle that binds us and makes life very difficult.

Objects like chairs have clear purposes and uses, but humans are born without a purpose and find themselves in the process of searching for one.

In that process, facing vague options with nothing settled and endlessly questioning the meaning of our existence is deeply painful, and actually leaves so many people depressed and powerless.

Then is a life without existential freedom, faithful to instinct, happier?

Is the life of a goldfish in a bowl happy?

Is it happy to act only on instinctive commands in response to what is before us, laughing in the happiness imposed by the resulting neurotransmitters and fleeing in anxiety?

I do not know. I think I too probably place a high value on intelligence and fear a life without even a choice.

Then all I can do is pursue the values I have created without doubting them, and love even the worries, anxiety, and pain that arise along the way.
