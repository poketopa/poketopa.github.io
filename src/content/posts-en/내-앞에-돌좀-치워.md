Recently, Ludovico said that if I lost control of my speed and seemed about to trip over a small stone and tumble several times, he would stop me.

<del><em><strong>He did mean that he would send my life crashing down when things were going well.</strong></em></del>

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Lately, I have been pushing myself to the limit while developing Knot.

Burnout came long ago, but I am running as though I could burn even the ashes left behind.

![](/images/rilog/276/01.webp "Even one little stone could send my heart flying like Lucci getting punched in the face by Luffy.")

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

The Knot team's project itself started late.

It was not until Level 4 that we scrapped the plan and began development.

Considering other teams launched their services in Level 3, it was a very late start.

Even so, we deployed our first product about six days ago.

Every teammate really burned through everything and pushed hard.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

I thought deploying would make me feel at least a little proud, but there was no time for that.

I already thought we were lacking when we deployed, but seeing real feedback come in made me feel the pressure of how far short we still fell.

All I could think about was quickly filling the gaps.

After deployment, the work to do doubled.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Amid this, I had another major goal: the Woowa Brothers exhibition.

<em>(Did you know that the Korean names 우아한형제들 and 우아한테크코스 have no spaces?)</em>

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

I spent the entire year at Woowacourse feeling lost.

I had so many shortcomings, and although I worked hard, I did not feel much like I was growing.

Because Woowacourse did not give us the right answers.

There was no standard by which to judge whether I was doing well.

I had to choose what to study and where to invest my time myself.

The people ahead of me kept moving ahead.

I was running too, but everyone was running, so the distance did not seem to close.

It was hard to tell how much I had grown.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

In that situation, the exhibition felt like the finale of Woowacourse to me.

I thought it was a place where I could prove myself under the Woowacourse name.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Once that thought took hold, I felt myself sinking further into it.

Woowacourse taught me that in development, every choice has tradeoffs, and there are no wrong choices.

So I tried to focus on what a choice had let me learn, rather than regret what I had not chosen.

That was true of the missions and of our late-starting project.

I did not regret the delay because it meant we had thought and learned more.

But if we missed out on the exhibition without my having given everything, I felt I would regret it terribly.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

There is no standard for how far I have to go to say I have given enough.

Because the moment I entertain 'What if we fail?', I feel my resolve might fade.

Right now, there is only 'do it.'

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

The sad part is that I am not running alone.

At every retrospective, I feel my teammates growing more exhausted.

They all said they were tired from pushing themselves so hard, mentally and physically.

They said they might burn out if things went wrong.

It is a natural result.

Everyone ran incredibly hard to bring this late-starting project this far.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Yet my ambition only keeps growing.

Even if I burned out after giving everything, I wanted to push harder for the few weeks remaining.

So I expressed my feelings actively, but I think my approach may have been somewhat coercive.

From my teammates' perspective, how could they push back against someone saying we should put worries about knowledge and technical debt aside for a while and keep running for the exhibition?

The more I showed how desperately I wanted it, the harder it might have been for them to say anything else.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

It is selfish.

I know it is blind and obsessive too.

Still, I want to give everything I have.

I do not yet know how to sort out this feeling.

My teammates' exhaustion and my desire to push harder are both facts of the present.

Writing a retrospective does not make either feeling disappear.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

But when this time ends, I want to tell my teammates that I am really, really grateful.

If the result is good, I will be able to say it with a happy smile; if not, we may need a little time to rest,

But when we get back up and run again, maybe we will be able to do anything.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

<strong>Yuru-i-heuk-neot, let's go\~</strong>
