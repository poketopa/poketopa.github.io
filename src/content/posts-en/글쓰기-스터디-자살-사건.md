> That day, the snowman lay in an empty bathtub. <br />Before turning on the hot water, he thought carefully about whether he should keep living. <br />Having no reason to keep living could not be a reason to die, and having no reason to die could not be a reason to live either. <br />There was no reason to die and no reason to keep living.<br /><br />When you are lying alone in an empty bathtub for no reason at all, which should you turn on: the hot water or the cold? <br />The snowman thought the outcome would be the same. <br />He would melt quickly in hot water and a little more slowly in cold water, but there was no difference in that he would melt away.<br /><br />I want to melt in warm water. <br />Haven't I spent far too long living in the cold? <br />The snowman turned on the hot water and fell asleep while watching his body gradually melt into water. <br />Steam billowed from the bathtub.<br /><br />\- A Snowman's Suicide (Daraesil, 2019)

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

I started a write-one-piece-a-day study group.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

We had agreed to do the study group, but had not decided when to start writing.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

It was already past midnight. When Moa asked when we would start writing, I thought it did not really matter.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Then the poem above suddenly came to mind.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

It stayed with me for a long time because the failure to find an answer in either reasons to keep living or reasons to die seemed to reflect the helplessness of people living today.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

At moments like this, when I had to decide something, I had often thought it did not matter whether it was today, tomorrow, or Eden from the eighth backend cohort. 

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Could all those complacent moments I dismissed as unimportant accumulate and eventually return as a sense of helplessness, as though I had lost the power to decide my own life?

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Even a life that feels as though I did not choose it may be the result of continually putting off choices I thought I could take or leave.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Starting by writing down these late-night musings, I want to keep my life from melting away too quickly.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>
