Yi Sang's Wings.

It was so difficult that I felt I could not absorb any of it, and I gave up after several attempts. Recently, the YouTube channel Neojinttok posted a video explaining it, so I reread the book, watched the video, and am recording my thoughts.

The novel's message concerns criticism of a half-formed modernism and growth through realization.

Modernism refers to an era of rapid industrialization grounded in science, objectivity, experience, reason, freedom, innovation, and criticism. Based on the idea that "All humans are rational, and rationality can solve any problem," it places progress and efficiency above all else.

Modernism opposed the imposition of religion and belief, but as it became entrenched over time, it began to degenerate into imposing a system (reason and practicality). As a result, among modernism's foundational elements, science, progress, and innovation became fixed, confined to following predetermined paths within a rigid framework, while freedom, innovation, and criticism lost their power for being 'irrational.'

The novel's opening line, "Do you know the genius who has become a stuffed specimen?", refers to people trapped inside a framework in a modernism that has lost its meaning, forced into reason and practicality and simply following the path they were taught.

The protagonist and his wife, who try to express their sincerity only by exchanging money; the protagonist, who cannot judge for himself even after discovering sleeping pills and cannot bring himself to question his wife; and the goldfish-like people he looks down on from the department store are all depicted as people caged and turned into stuffed specimens.

In a world that has lost romance, daring, criticism, and innovation, the protagonist searches for meaning and hears a siren announcing noon from atop the department store. Midnight is the frightening hour that ruined his relationship with his wife, but noon is the same hour and yet its exact opposite, bathed in bright sunlight.

Through this, the protagonist discovers the existence of modernism's missing half and resolves to throw away his artificial wings and try flying once more on new ones.

Hearing this interpretation gave me chills. I had not realized how much an author's characteristics and historical background could shape our understanding of a work. Of course, there is no single answer when interpreting literature, so I do not know whether the interpretation above is right, but discovering a message I had never imagined was a great pleasure.

I was especially drawn to lines from Yi Sang's other works

> In the Ginza sky after the advertising balloon has landed, stars may still glitter through God's consideration, yet these descendants of Cain have long since forgotten the stars.

Advertising balloon = technological progress

Stars = the values of true modernism

Descendants of Cain = people living in modernism

> These citizens, taught to fear poison gas more than Noah's flood, quite candidly take the subway together on their way home from a stroll.

Poison gas = technological progress and education

Subway = a product of technology that keeps us from seeing the stars

Being able to write things like this—surely this is what genius is.

I too want to live a life more successful and prosperous than others, but I should always remember Yi Sang's words so that I do not lose more important values along the way.
