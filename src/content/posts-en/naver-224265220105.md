The premise running throughout the book was that “people are inherently selfish, emotional, and irrational.”

The idea is that the other person is not an individual equal to or more capable than me, but a selfish, emotional animal, so a good relationship is easy to obtain as long as I anticipate and provide what they want.

The wording was somewhat aggressive, but I did not dislike it.

If that attitude offers practical help in maintaining good relationships, there is no reason to reject it.

I found it interesting, and liked, the observations that a soup stain on one's clothes is more troubling to a person than a famine with a million casualties,

that when people have their mistakes pointed out, their first response is to resent being corrected rather than accept the criticism,

and that people are very forgiving toward someone who is diligent about criticizing themselves.

Still, some of what I experienced at Woowacourse ran counter to the book, which gave me a chance to approach the issue from a different direction.

For a relationship that goes beyond getting along and involves wanting each other to grow, I felt it was right to acknowledge each other as equals, honestly point out shortcomings, assess them objectively, and help make up for each other's weaknesses and encourage individual growth.

Considering the author's profession and the circumstances of his time, I thought the book presents its methods strictly for business purposes, focusing on satisfying others and gaining a good reputation in the short term rather than on long-term growth and relationships that help both people develop.

Even so, its methods seemed very useful for casual relationships or the beginning of a new relationship, so I found it an interesting read.

> A person determined to make the most of their abilities has no time to waste on personal arguments, much less room to accept consequences such as hurt feelings and a loss of self-control. If you and the other person are almost equally right, yield even on a major issue. Even when you are clearly right, yield if the issue is trivial. It is better to let a dog have the path than to be bitten arguing over your right to it. Killing the dog will not heal the bite.
