I expected philosophy, but this was about neuroscience.

Everything in the world is a product of chance, yet people try to interpret chance as fate.

Because the human brain fears uncertainty and struggles to accept things without a reason.

There were many sad stories (reality checks), but I liked how it lightly explored topics I had been thinking about lately.

> Topics that stayed with me
>
> What determines whether someone becomes a great figure or an unsuccessful homeless person is simply chance.
>
> Because most things, such as our surroundings and opportunities, are beyond our control, individual ability plays a smaller part in success than we think.
>
> (In fact, individual ability itself is ultimately formed by chance.)
>
> When waiting in line at the supermarket, it feels as though only the other lines move quickly, but with even just three lines, the probability that mine moves quickly is only one in three.
>
> People struggle to accept events without a reason (accidents or deaths involving people around them) and want to know why tragedy happened specifically to them. But it was always nearby; they simply could not see it because they had not experienced it.

Maybe life is ultimately a game of rock-paper-scissors. People who fall short were simply unlucky.

I do not know what fate has in store for me, but I am certain that I can improve things if I make an effort.
