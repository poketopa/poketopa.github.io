At Woowa Tech Course, I felt I was learning every day. I liked having an environment where I could study as much as I wanted, receive reviews, and change my thinking.

But at the same time, I also felt impatient. I wrote this in my March retrospective.

> It made me impatient to see other people comfortably handling object-oriented concepts that I did not know, as though they were basic knowledge.

Even when I learned something new that day, seeing another crew member use it naturally made me worry about my pace.

The more I study, the more I feel I know nothing, and the gap between me and those ahead of me does not seem to shrink.

It is hard to shake that feeling. For now, I should focus on my own growth.

## Focusing on my own growth

From Blackjack to Janggi, I was one of the last people whose work was merged.

At first, I felt embarrassed. It was not as though I had not studied, yet I fell behind on missions and was unsure whether I was doing well.

I thought more deeply about ideas from the study group and tried to focus on my own growth rather than other people's pace. I wrote this in my April retrospective.

> I felt more at ease after finding a way to focus entirely on my own growth rather than speed.

It really helped at the time. Studying on my own and managing my time also felt a little easier.

But that thought did not eliminate my impatience.

## The more I learn, the more I feel I lack

When I started Level 2, I expected that by the end I would be ready to prepare for the project.

But in my June retrospective, I wrote that I felt more inadequate than when I started.

It was not that I had learned nothing. I could explain why domain responsibilities should be divided in terms of difficulties in my own code, and I thought more concretely about tests and failure responses.

But the things I realized I had not known seemed to grow faster than the things I was learning.

I had expected that learning more would make me feel less inadequate. Instead, learning allowed me to see shortcomings that had previously been invisible.

Even when I tried not to compare myself to others, my work seemed small again against these newly visible standards.

The fact that I am growing and the feeling that I am doing well enough do not always arrive together.

Gump said that there will always be crew members who are better than you in some area, but most people struggle because they combine all those strengths into an imaginary superhuman and compare themselves to that person.

I think I do that too.

When I actually asked the other crew members, they said they felt impatient because someone who had started from scratch had caught up so close behind them. Yet I still feel anxious about not being able to surpass them.

It will not be easy, but let me become someone who can love growth itself.
