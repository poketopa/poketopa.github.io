## The problem

Our team was often asked, `Why should I use this service when Clova Note already exists?`<br />Even putting other features aside, we believed <strong>transcription quality needed to be at least on par with other transcription services.</strong>

So we worked to improve transcription quality and speaker diarization.<br />This post records the errors we found, what we changed, and the criteria we used to select the final configuration.

The work falls broadly into two areas.

1. <strong>Creating ground truth and evaluating quality</strong>
2. <strong>Restoring the timeline and improving speaker attribution</strong>

## 1\. Creating ground truth

Before evaluating, we needed <strong>ground truth</strong>.<br />To say something had “improved,” we needed a correct reference.

Reading the transcripts, they felt more natural than before.<br />But <strong>natural sentences and an accurate record of what was actually said were different things.</strong>

`There was a problem.` `There was no problem.`

As in these examples, changing just one similar-sounding expression can produce natural sentences with opposite meanings. A single changed number or negation could alter how a meeting decision was read.

Having AI grade the naturalness of the text could not tell us whether it was correct.<br />“There was a problem.” “There was no problem.”<br />AI judges both to be natural sentences.

Ultimately, we had to test by listening to the audio ourselves.<br />But listening to and transcribing every file would take too much time, so we built an <strong>HTML comparison screen</strong> and listened only to passages where Clova Note and the Knot model disagreed, deciding which was correct.

Through this work, we produced ground truth for <strong>about 30 hours</strong> of recordings.

![Transcription ground-truth review screen](/images/rilog/362/01.webp)

Of course, this method had limitations. If Clova Note and the Knot model produced exactly the same incorrect transcription, we could not catch it. But we judged that unlikely and believed a large amount of ground truth would help improve performance.

## 2\. Evaluating quality

After creating the ground truth, we compared results while changing models and execution settings.<br />We were willing to use a little more of the Mac mini server's resources if it improved transcription quality.

For text, we counted substitutions, deletions, and insertions relative to the reference and compared <strong>character error rates</strong>.

- Exclude spaces and punctuation.
- Evaluate speaker identification separately.
- Match human-labeled speakers to model speakers and compare the amount of speech time assigned to the correct person.

### How did other models perform?

First, we checked whether we could replace our current `Whisper large-v3` model.<br />We transcribed entire files with `large-v3-turbo` and ran large-v3 with the same method and context settings.<br />Across the evaluation sections of five recordings, character error rates were <strong>8.9% for large-v3 and 11.9% for turbo</strong>. On this material, switching to turbo offered no quality advantage.

We also ran an `f16` model instead of the `Q5_0` quantized model we were using.<br />This tested whether representing model values at higher precision would improve the results.<br />On a separate evaluation section, f16 did not perform better.<br />We also applied `Qwen3-ASR` to the same evaluation section, but its error rate was higher than our existing Whisper configuration.

Within the material and settings we compared, <strong>simply replacing the model or increasing precision did not improve quality.</strong>

### Even the same model produces different results with different settings

We also compared whether to use previously transcribed text as context for the next transcription, and whether to retry with different settings when recognition was uncertain. Even with the same model file, these conditions changed omissions and repetitions.

When transcribing entire files, disabling previous context reduced errors.<br />But applying the same setting to every section when transcribing in chunks actually increased errors.<br />Disabling `temperature fallback`, which retries with different settings when recognition is uncertain, also made the results worse.

We needed to check whether a setting that improved one recording also helped other recordings or processing methods. When comparing models and settings, we used the same inputs and ground truth wherever possible, and recorded any differences in processing conditions beyond the model.

## 3\. Assigning speakers

Once we checked that speech recognition was accurate, it was time to identify who said what.<br />There were many problems here, too. Words were transcribed correctly but attributed to another person, or the same person became a different speaker in each section.

### Different time references for words and speakers

To assign speakers to words, we compared word timestamps with speaker timestamps.<br />But <strong>the two used different time references.</strong><br />At the time, we were directly comparing word timestamps produced after silence removal in `whisper.cpp` with speaker timestamps analyzed from the original audio.

If three seconds of earlier silence were removed, speech at ten seconds in the original could appear at seven seconds in the processed audio.<br />Comparing that seven-second timestamp with the original speaker intervals could attribute it to someone else.

![](/images/rilog/362/02.webp)

So we modified whisper.cpp to <strong>restore word timestamps to the original audio timeline</strong>.<br />For audio processed in chunks, we also added each chunk's starting offset to align it with the full meeting timeline before assigning speakers.

### Speaker numbers changing between sections

A person labeled A in one section was not necessarily A in the next.<br />Connecting section-level numbers directly could split one person into multiple speakers or mix different people's speech.

So we collected voice features extracted with `ERes2Net` from each section and <strong>regrouped similar utterances across the entire meeting.</strong><br />We wanted to compare actual voice features instead of connecting people simply because their numbers matched.

## 4\. Comparing with commercial services

Ultimately, Knot's accuracy improved to a level similar to other transcription services.<br />The experiment evaluated <strong>about 10 hours</strong> of discussions about development, medicine, and public health, comparing services using <strong>the same audio and ground truth</strong>.<br />Since reference character counts differed by recording, we used character accuracy weighted by the number of reference characters.

| Service | Character accuracy |
| --- | --- |
| <strong>Knot</strong> | <strong>91.81%</strong> |
| Clova Note | 93.29% |
| Daglo | 92.93% |
| Aiko | 89.60% |
| Apple Voice Memos | 69.82% |
