## If I were to guess why people say, "People do not change,"

It would be because changing someone's thinking and persuading them is so difficult without strong motivation they have generated themselves through a personal event, realization, or something similar.

From that perspective, I think teaching or preaching to others is likely to fail unless you convince them of "why" they should act—the motivation behind the action.

Drawing on philosophy and neuroscience, this book explains why people need to experience flow in life and how it can change their lives and guide them in a better direction.

The book broadly divides what we do outside eating and sleeping into three categories.

Things we have to do

Things we want to do

Things we could take or leave

People feel the most depressed when doing the third kind: things they could take or leave.

They report feeling depressed when they spend time doing things that feel like neither work nor play.

While doing physically and mentally exhausting work, we want to quit our jobs and enjoy freedom if we are given money and time. Paradoxically, however, humans experience their greatest suffering from that very freedom (being left to themselves).

Whether freely chosen or imposed, working at the responsibilities we are given and investing time in them—the very things we want so badly to escape—allows us to experience joy through repeated small goals and achievements.

And this joy varies from person to person, depending on their ability to concentrate and enter flow.

People who can achieve greater flow gain greater accomplishment and higher self-esteem.

This is why humans need to experience flow.

---

I think goals like "become an amazing person everyone looks up to" or "become a top-level elite in some field" ultimately cannot bring happiness through achievement.

Goals based on some metric may drive growth, but since a person's level of thinking can be determined by genetics and environment, I think they will eventually hit a limit and become discouraged regardless of their will.

So I think loving human growth and the process itself is a worthy meaning of life.

In that sense, I enjoyed this book because I felt that what it said about flow resonated with my own values.
