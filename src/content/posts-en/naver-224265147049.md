Of the short stories, “Headlights” stays with me most. (Honestly, I don't remember the others.)

The protagonist runs toward the “good life” we pursue.

Yet the process is so smooth and dry that it feels deeply unsettling.

The reason becomes clear toward the end: rather than truly achieving the goals, the protagonist has revised and rationalized them to make it seem as though they were achieved.

The protagonist's life—taking a job that looks respectable to others, choosing relationships encountered by chance along the way, and dating someone who misses every quality of the desired ideal partner—is packaged as a hollow success.

But I could not easily criticize the protagonist, knowing how much self-censorship and effort it takes to maintain the normality society demands.

> He blew out the candles and clapped in the darkness.

It is easy to laugh at the protagonist, but I felt a creeping fear that I, too, might be deceiving myself as I clap in the darkness.
