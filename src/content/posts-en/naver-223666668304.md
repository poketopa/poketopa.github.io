It felt like the quintessential self-help book.

Perhaps its message is utterly obvious, or perhaps it cuts to the truth of life.

Success does not come from ideas.

Anyone can succeed by devoting spare time entirely to studying and putting plans into practice one by one.

Lives diverge depending on how much people act on that thought.

Because it is something everyone knows,

“This is just obvious stuff anyone could say,”

or

“Why was I lying in bed just now when I want to succeed?” seem like the two kinds of responses it might produce,

and I think that explains why people are so divided about self-help books.

Here are some remarks that impressed me.

> Thoroughly avoid the behavior and thinking of poor people.
>
> First increase your market value and earn money, then invest that money.
>
> What matters is life itself, not money and fame.
>
> Do not be swayed by social media and work-life balance.
>
> Do not compare yourself with others.
>
> A problem you cannot answer within ten minutes is not one you can solve.
>
> Most worries already have an answer.
>
> A problem you cannot answer after ten minutes of thought cannot be solved right now, so it becomes needless worry.
>
> To succeed, accumulate money rather than memories when you are young.
>
> Read books that do not help you earn money, too, to develop insight into life.
>
> Winners start running and calculate along the way, while losers are busy calculating before they even start.

Most of this aligns with my values.

But “To succeed, accumulate money rather than memories when you are young” has recently been my biggest dilemma.

Because money grows through compound interest, I think it is important to accumulate meaningful seed capital as early as possible.

So I thought saving the money I would spend traveling while young was the way to accumulate money quickly.

Time is one of the most important things in life, and gaining wealth while even a day younger is a way to save precious time.

Then a thought suddenly occurred to me.

In that case, how precious are trips and memories made while young?

It feels so difficult to find a balance between memories made in youth and future wealth.

We cannot buy back time with money, but selling time to buy money is miserable, too.

It feels like such a difficult question.
