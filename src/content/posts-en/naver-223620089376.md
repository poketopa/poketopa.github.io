The garden is where Craven, the owner of the manor, lost his beloved wife.

To him, I think it represents hurt, regret, and anguish.

After losing his wife in the garden, Craven locked its gate and buried the key somewhere.

I saw this as burying the wounds of the past and avoiding the feelings they bring out of fear.

He no longer tried to look into the garden, and came to wish his son Colin had never been born.

But when he hears his wife's voice in a dream and steps into the garden again, he sees Colin and the children running around.

At that moment, he realizes the garden has become a happy place where the children make memories and grow.

The garden has become a symbol of recovery and new beginnings, rather than simply a symbol of pain.

I thought this resembled facing the wounds of the past and finding hope in life again through them.

Rather than burying the sad, painful wounds we experience in life,

I thought facing them and discovering new lessons, meaning, and value was a healthy way of responding to suffering.

Facing pain rather than looking away from it, and finding new lessons and hope within it, is a healthy approach to life.

> It was the fact that thoughts alone can be as powerful as an electric battery,
>
> and can be as beneficial as sunlight or as harmful as poison.
