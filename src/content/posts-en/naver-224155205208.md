## It was so immersive that reading the novel felt like watching an action film.

**I was impressed by how the specialized knowledge introduced and explained in the story was astonishingly deep yet easy to understand.**

**For example, the novel predicts that a superintelligence could instantly understand difficult phenomena whose causal relationships humans cannot easily grasp.**

## Unsolved problems such as the Riemann hypothesis and the three-body problem would be examples of those difficult phenomena.

**I wondered how the novel could convincingly show that a superintelligent being, only three years old and barely able to speak, had the potential to solve difficult problems easily and the capacity to threaten humanity.**

**Weather is said to be extremely difficult to predict accurately because variables smaller than the smallest numerical unit a computer can handle can drastically change the outcome.**

**In the novel, a scene in which the young superintelligent being draws a small circle on the ground, stands up, and drops a leaf so that it lands precisely inside the circle demonstrates that, despite its young age, it can foresee things beyond what humans can calculate.**

**There were quite a few scenes like this, expressing in simple terms subjects that would otherwise require deep specialist knowledge to explain, and they provided a particular pleasure in reading this book.**

**In particular, there was a lot about computers and security, and its accuracy and persuasiveness made it surprising and enjoyable.**

## My only disappointment was the insistence on spelling out the theme's meaning.

## Genocide: the deliberate or systematic elimination of all or part of a national, ethnic, racial, or religious group

## I read while questioning the title Genocide and wondering what it meant.

**As I read, speculating whether it meant genocide for the sake of a single superhuman or the massacres humans inflict on other humans, the author began using the characters' words late in the story to emphasize and criticize the theme of genocide and human violence very directly and repeatedly.**

## This felt as though it shut down the space for my own imagination, leaving me disappointed after reading.

> Tears came as he felt a guilt that would never leave him for the rest of his life. Angered by the fragility of life, by the chillingly horrific side of humanity, by the powerlessness of goodness, and by himself, unable even to judge good from evil, Jaeger wept bitterly without making a sound.
