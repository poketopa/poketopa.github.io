I had picked this out to read, and when I recently visited Youngpoong Bookstore, it was a bestseller

The military library really does seem to buy only bestsellers

It says to build your seed money as quickly as possible, and that the moment your capital income exceeds your earned income is the day you become financially independent and wealthy

Rather than explaining various perspectives, the book felt as though it was pushing the author's philosophy quite forcefully

Its message was to rule out unstable investments, choose the number one or two stocks, and take it slowly, looking ahead to your forties and fifties

There were a few bits that made me go, hmm...

Statements like "I do not invest in vaccine companies because they have to hope people die"

And the fact that "We should take care of things built with taxes because everyone's money paid for them"

Appears in the same book as "If you just earn money without thinking, taxes will take it all away"?

Because this was a book by someone who had reached such a high level, I took its lessons to heart, but I could not help feeling a little resistance as I read

The book was somewhat removed from my own thinking, but precisely because of that, it helped rein in my greed and made me face reality again
