Feedback is really difficult.

Because I think the phrase `so that improvements can be made or action taken` in its definition is very abstract and applies differently to each person.

Here is an example.

I can honestly point out that someone's habit, behavior, or way of thinking is terrible.

If they receive that purely as an observation rather than an attack, they can reflect on themselves and grow.

But how many people can make that ideal judgment?

What if their negative feelings about being judged and treated with hostility come before consideration of their own mistake?

The feedback becomes meaningless, and the relationship is also likely to worsen.

In fact, How to Win Friends and Influence People, which Gump recommended, says this.

```text
Criticism is futile because it puts a person on the defensive and usually makes him strive to justify himself.

비판은 대개 소용이 없다. 사람을 방어적으로 만들고, 자기 잘못을 인정하기보다 자신을 정당화하게 만들기 때문이다.
```

In other words, the emotional response to feedback comes before rational judgment, easily distorting its meaning.

I do not think I need to convey my honest feelings at the risk of damaging relationships, so in most situations, rather than say what I truly think, I consider what sweet words the other person most wants to hear right now and say those.

But after taking various soft-skills classes and reading books at Woowacourse, I began to think that this was never a way for us to truly help each other grow.

In fact, in my review of How to Win Friends and Influence People, which I read on Gump's recommendation, I wrote this.

```text
개인의 옷에 튄 국물 자국은 백만 명의 사상자를 낸 기근보다 신경 쓰이는 것이며,

인간은 본래 자신의 실수에 대해 지적받았을 때, 수용하기보다 자신의 실수를 지적받았다는 사실에 분개하고,

자아비판을 열심히 하는 사람에게 매우 관대하다는 그런 말들이 흥미롭고 좋았다.

다만, 내가 우테코에서 느꼈던 것이 책에서 말하는 것과 반대인 부분이 존재한다는 것이 다른 방향으로 발상을 시도해 볼 수 있는 부분이었다.

단지 좋은 인간관계를 넘어 서로의 성장을 바라는 관계라면, 서로 대등함을 인정하고 부족한 점을 솔직하게 지적하며

그것에 대해 객관적으로 판단하여 서로의 부족한 점들을 보완하고, 개인의 성장을 이끌어내는 것이 옳다고 느꼈기 때문이다.
```

Sweet words are useful enough in bringing people closer and relieving someone's immediate situation, but I thought they could not be positive in the long run because they cannot solve the fundamental problems I see in the other person.

If I genuinely want to grow together with someone, I have to convey words that could cut like a dagger.

The hardest part was weighing what feedback to give, how to give it, to whom, and in what circumstances.

I tried various things at Woowacourse.

Sometimes I simply thought about what sweet words the other person most wanted to hear and said something kind. At other times, I honestly shared my true feelings, accepting that they might dislike me for it.

In the end, most thanked me for giving good feedback.

Of course, those who felt otherwise probably would not have gone out of their way to tell me something negative, so it is hard to judge...

From what I can tell, my balancing act has been fairly successful so far.

I will keep trying to become someone who helps the people around me grow more.
