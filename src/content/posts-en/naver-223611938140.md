## The Proof of Gu

---

I reread The Proof of Gu for a book club.

I remember feeling more shock than appreciation after my first reading.

I found its descriptions and expression too provocative and cruel, leaving only unpleasant feelings.

I could not understand why it was a bestseller.

Here, I have written my impressions after rereading it more than a year later.

## Impressions on a second reading

The thought that would not leave my mind as I reread it was that I could relate to the protagonists' emotions.

I could deeply relate to Gu and Dam's monologues about each other and the chilling depictions of emotions that some might condemn.

The book expresses, without filtering, thoughts we might have when emotions in a romantic relationship reach an extreme, feelings we cannot easily share with others. I related to this strongly, and thought it explained the book's bestseller status.

Only realizing this upon rereading probably meant I had gone through many experiences and changes in emotion myself.

## What does “proof” mean in The Proof of Gu?

These are my thoughts on the question, “What is meant to prove Gu?”

“Do I wish to be human?” Dam says this near the end of the novel while eating Gu.

I went back over the book, wondering what it meant to wish to be human when she already was.

Dam says, “Does a mature adult calmly accept death? If so, I never want to become a mature adult.”

Dam can never accept Gu's death, and so thinks she does not want to be human, to be a mature adult.

I thought this was Dam's thinking behind the phrase “the proof of Gu.”

Unable to accept Gu's death, Dam probably believes that eating him makes him part of the same being as herself, so her being alive itself proves Gu's existence.

## Can the cannibalism in the novel be justified?

Dam loves Gu too much to lose him, and eats him in a mixture of anger and grief over his unjust death.

I thought that from nature's perspective, cannibalism was not something to condemn.

Yet it is true that I feel an inner aversion to it.

In the novel, Dam says that strong animals can protect themselves, but humans without money cannot.

She also tells the story of a tribe, saying that a child of the tribe has practiced cannibalism since birth and feels no guilt about it.

Until the child was executed, they reportedly wondered why others hated and criticized them.

Apart from this, Dam tells Gu she wants to drift forever through a universe where human rules mean nothing.

Based on these statements, here is what I thought about the author's message.

In nature, animals eat other animals or members of their own species, and humans sometimes give live prey to their pets.

Asked whether cannibalism can be judged negatively from that perspective, I might ask what the problem is,

but even I feel an aversion to cannibalism deep down, so I can never think positively about it.

I think education explains this difference between deeply rooted feelings and conscious thought.

Not only knowledge but even preferences and likes and dislikes rooted deep in the human heart are instilled through education, leading to unconscious hatred of certain things.

I think such prejudices and aversions are instilled through education because the things they target can often cause social disorder. But surely some of them may be indoctrinated to benefit particular groups or control a population.

I thought looking at events from outside the cognition formed by education was important for recognizing these things, and wondered whether that was part of the author's intent.

> Children give even objects a personality, but adults treat even humans as objects.
