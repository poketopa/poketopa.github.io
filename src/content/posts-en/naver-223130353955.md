Sometimes a thought suddenly occurs to me.

“Where do I end?”

My mind? My brain? My body?

Or even the environment and people around me?

I once watched the Crayon Shin-chan movie about the robot dad

and got chills from its premise.

One day, Shin-chan's dad wakes up feeling strangely light.

He goes to work as usual, but people are horrified to see him.

Puzzled, he goes home, only to find another Shin-chan's dad there.

When he looks in the mirror, he sees that he is a robot.

Shin-chan's dad and the robot dad: which is real, and which is fake?

Can we say they are different when they branched out from the same memories?

Because “knowing” is the foundation of every relationship in the world,

what matters is the mind, memories, and stories.

People long to live for eternity and understand infinite knowledge and the principles of the world, but humans are mortal in the end.

Yet because we shine briefly and fade, we can feel and learn from even the little things around us.

Not a happy, failed, moving, or depressing story,

but simply my story.

> They were all odes to mortal humans. Without the undertone of life's finiteness, there was neither emotion nor resonance. Because they were beings who had to die, because they had only one life, everything mattered so desperately to humans.
