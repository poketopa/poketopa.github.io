I didn't know who Lee O-young was, so I wasn't particularly curious about the book, but learning that he was the first minister of culture and the person who created Hanyego piqued my interest, and I read it.

(Though valuing who did something more than what they did seems like a bad habit…)

I liked learning some wisdom about life, particularly the attitude with which we accept death as human beings.

Still, you only see as much as you know, and quite a few expressions in the book did not really reach me.

I could sense profound meaning in them, but was frustrated that I could not understand it.

I think rereading it later will feel different.
