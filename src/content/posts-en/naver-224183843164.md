Personal growth caught my attention more than the story of excitement and love.

People have somewhat fixed ideas and tendencies, so I think it is difficult to quickly accept new ways of thinking or have unfamiliar experiences.

New things come from experience rather than thought, and turning thought into action through willpower alone is difficult.

In that sense, perhaps our opportunities to encounter new worlds depend on the friends or partners around us.

For example, suppose someone thinks it is more efficient to watch a short summary video and use the remaining time for self-improvement than to read a book. If they receive a book as a gift, read it, and finally become captivated by the emotions only a book can offer, they have received from someone else an unfamiliar experience and happiness they would have struggled to create on their own.

In the book, the male protagonist uses the phrase "leaf boat" to describe himself as someone swept along by his surroundings, but the female protagonist overturns that idea.

She tells him that every situation we experience now is something we have chosen and decided for ourselves, rather than something we were swept into from the past.

Understanding her meaning, he sincerely accepts the circumstances he has been given and his own feelings, and feels grateful.

I had recently read the philosophy book The Courage to Be Disliked and was thinking about various things. Seeing the protagonist regard the challenges before him entirely as his own decisions and try to take responsibility for them helped me understand a little more deeply what that philosophy book was trying to say.

I wondered whether philosophy might be the struggle to find happiness in what we have by shifting the way we see our circumstances, rather than changing the circumstances themselves.

> That is why it took a whole year. I suppose that is partly my responsibility, too?
>
> But at last, I have made it this far through my own choices. I would like you to praise me for that.
>
> A year ago, I made a clear choice. To become someone like you.
>
> Someone who knows how to acknowledge others, someone who knows how to love others.
>
> I still do not know whether I have succeeded, but at least I made that choice.
