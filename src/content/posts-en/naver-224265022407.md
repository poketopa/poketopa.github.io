## All worries arise from interpersonal relationships.

When I first saw this sentence, I felt a vague resistance.

I think I disliked it because it seemed to say that my concerns and countless questions did not originate within me, but were dependent on others.

Reading the book, however, I gradually came to understand the authors' argument (Adlerian psychology).

I realized that many worries and thoughts I believed arose from a complicated world did not actually exist in that form; instead, they formed because my wavering perspective left me no choice but to see the world as complicated.

---

## How to distinguish tasks

## Who bears the final outcome?

The one question that can untangle complicated relationships is “Whose task is this?”

We need to consider who ultimately bears the consequences of a choice, determine whose domain the action belongs to, and act accordingly.

My domain is deciding which values to choose, how to act, and committing to take responsibility for the outcome,

while the other person's domain is deciding whether to praise, criticize, or feel disappointed by my actions.

Most relationship worries arise from bringing other people's domains into my own and allowing them to unsettle my choices.

How someone responds to my actions is their decision and responsibility, so it is their burden to bear. My actions should not be swayed by their decision, nor should I anticipate it and make a different choice.

The reverse also applies: I should not try to control someone else's actions through my choices.

## Does that mean caring about nothing concerning each other and neglecting our relationships?

The point is not **“Be indifferent to others,”** but to understand that **you can lead a horse to water, but you cannot make it drink.**

My responsibility extends to letting the other person know I am always ready to help if they need it and honestly expressing my wishes. I must acknowledge that their subsequent choice belongs entirely to them,

and even if the outcome differs from what I hoped, recognize it as their choice rather than demanding another action from them or blaming myself for my choice.

Ultimately, we strive to meet others' expectations because we want approval. But we need to recognize that this is a life dependent on others and live autonomously (even if others dislike us).

Other people's judgments of me are simply their task. Only when I walk the path I believe is best can I escape the prison of other people.

---

I realized so much while reading, but it is not easy to put it into writing without making it difficult and vague.

I think sensitive people get hurt because they feel they have not received as much as they gave, and fear how others will judge their actions. As the book says, perhaps distinguishing my domain from others', doing my best with my own choices, and committing to taking responsibility could make me a stronger person.

Let's try.
