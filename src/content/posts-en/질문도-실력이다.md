I am not at all ashamed of not knowing something.

If I start from the premise `When I encounter something I do not know, I ask.`, the moment I discover something I do not know, I ask and learn the answer, so `not knowing` does not feel negative.

But that attitude alone does not seem to have been enough.

I realized that the other person can never know where my knowledge ends and my ignorance begins, and that without precise context from me, it is hard for them to teach me.

Looking back on Level 1, what I learned most was how to ask reviewers questions.

For someone who already knows a subject deeply, the thought process when looking at a problem is smooth and natural. So when another person asks about a problem, it is hard to immediately understand where in their thought process they got stuck.

> As experts repeat tasks, the process becomes automated, putting their learning process in the unconscious realm. They often cannot fully explain how they solve problems.
>
> We ask them 'How do you do it?' and they inevitably respond with things like 'Work hard.'
>
> Then how can I draw out the information I want from an expert—their way of learning?
>
> I have to extract the thinking processes happening in the expert's unconscious.
>
> Rather than simply asking 'What is your secret to learning programming languages quickly?', ask 'When you learned your most recent language, what did you do at each point in time?' and draw out the unconscious decisions and judgments hidden in the learning process, step by step through time.
> [Excerpt from my review of Growing Together](https://poketopa.github.io/posts/naver-224266923896/)

## Writing down opinions that might be wrong

Digging deeply into my answers to 'Why did you do it?' and 'Why did you not do it?', I think I usually answered `because I could not be bothered`.

[My reply](https://github.com/woowacourse/java-blackjack/pull/1138#discussion_r2951013284) explaining why I introduced DTOs in Blackjack Cycle 2 is still there.

> Previously, the view received Players and Dealer objects directly and printed them.
>
> I used that approach because I thought creating DTOs would be a burden while features were continually being added, but (honestly, I think that was an excuse I came up with because I could not be bothered)

My understanding might be wrong, or the grounds for judging something good might be shallow.

But clearly exposing what I know and what I do not know will be much more useful in getting the knowledge I want than simply saying I am unsure.

I thought I needed to go one step beyond not hiding what I do not know.

It seems I have to lay out even what I think I know to learn what I have misunderstood.
