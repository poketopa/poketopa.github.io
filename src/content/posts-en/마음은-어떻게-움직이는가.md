[Watch The Paradox of Choice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X71gvsqMk4k)

This video discusses the paradox of choice.

The more choices we have, the less satisfied we become with our choice and the more likely we are to regret it.

When I watched it before, several people around me came to mind.

They knew more than I did, had more experiences and knew of more options, so when faced with a choice, they weighed far more possibilities than I could even consider.

I wanted to tell them what might help in those situations, so I really wanted to read this book.

But rather than describing one phenomenon and its solutions, the book introduced assorted snippets of psychology, which disappointed me, and I did not find answers to those questions.

Instead, I looked through the contents and read only the chapters that interested me. I especially loved one called “Try to explain love, and it will cool.”

It said that emotions cannot be adequately expressed, and that because what we can explain is standardized, attempts to explain emotions simplify things that are enormously complex.

In other words, the moment we try to describe an emotion, we damage it.

I have always tried to organize my emotions in writing and in words.

Because I disliked how treasured experiences faded over time, eventually leaving only an abstract sense that “it was nice.”

I thought only when I could logically explain why I liked or disliked something would my happiness become objective, allowing me to explain it to others, too.

Perhaps my love and memories had been fading from the moment I began searching for reasons behind the things I love.

Below are thoughts I shared with fellow participants about this, which was a truly meaningful experience.

It made me want to try exchanging books with others.
