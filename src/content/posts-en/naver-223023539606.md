A book about the lessons the author received as a child from his own father and his friend's father, and which lessons were right or wrong.

(His own father is the poor dad; his friend's father is the rich dad.)

In general, the book argues that you should accumulate money as “cleverly” and “shrewdly” as possible.

It says the path to wealth is to invest as much of your income and assets as possible into capital, minimize taxes, build a structure that earns money through capital income, and grow your assets through investing.

Conversely, if you want to become poor, it says to cling to earned income, lose half of it to taxes, and remain a slave to your paycheck until old age.

The circumstances of the time (Japanese real estate) may have helped the author make a fortune, but even without that, he is undoubtedly an impressive and bold capitalist.

As you would expect from such a bold author, the book gave me plenty to think about and taught me a lot.

It was quite entertaining and instilled ambition in me.

Still, I thought I needed to find new answers for myself because it is quite removed from present-day reality.
