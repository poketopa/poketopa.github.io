I thought I was good at contributing opinions in team activities.

If I thought differently, I said so, and I asked about things I did not understand. I believed that examining things critically before making a decision helped the team.

But reflecting on leadership and followership at Woowacourse, I discovered something a little different.

I was active in expressing my opinion, but I was not sure I stayed just as active after my opinion was not chosen.

In the May 28 assignment, I wrote this.

> I discovered that when the chosen direction differs from my thinking, I tend to lose some motivation and fail to participate actively.

Maybe I was only giving opinions about things I wanted to do.

## My attitude toward disappointing decisions

When I think one approach is better but the decision goes in another direction, I feel disappointed.

If I participated in the discussion and fully expressed my thoughts, I at least want to hear a reason that would make me feel my thinking was wrong.

But the team's decision cannot always wait until I am fully convinced.

That sense of helplessness chips away at my motivation. It can become an obstacle to the team's progress, but it was hard to manage my feelings.

Thinking about it the other way around, I really dislike this attitude too. As a team leader, seeing a teammate lose motivation because their opinion was not adopted would be very upsetting.

I do not want to be that teammate. I want a team that trusts its leader and does its best even when it disagrees, and I want to become that person too.

## Trying the follower role

In a soft-skills workshop, I wrote that the role I wanted to try was a committed follower.

I said I wanted to trust the leader's decision and immerse myself in it.

Reading that alone, it might look like a promise to abandon my opinions and follow unconditionally.

But I attached a condition to the action I would actually try.

Ask why the decision was made, and if it is reasonably rational, commit to it.

I think I wanted to accept that even if it is not my favorite answer, it might be a reasonable answer for the team to choose.

Before a decision, I can question and challenge it thoroughly. Afterward, I need to find what I can do to help that direction work.

I simply do not want to participate in the project while continuing to stand by just because the answer was not the one I chose.

In the project starting soon, I want to try being a teammate who respects every decision and follows it with all my effort.
