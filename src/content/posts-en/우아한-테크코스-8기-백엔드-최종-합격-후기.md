# _**"Woowa Tech Course Cohort 8 Web Backend Crew"**_

<br/><br/>

---

After a precourse and coding test that felt both short and long, I was finally accepted into Woowa Tech Course Cohort 8.

I want to write a comprehensive retrospective covering the personal statement, precourse, reflection essays, open mission, coding test, and more.

Just as acceptance stories on other blogs helped me prepare, I hope this post helps someone in the future, so I will also include tips from my own perspective.

# Introduction
### The most important part of selection
I think writing is by far the most important part of the Woowa Tech Course selection process.

The guidance and messages throughout the process suggest that they want to select people who can become good at coding, rather than people who are already good.

And their criteria will be the keywords Woowa Tech Course values: challenge, immersion, and metacognition.

I felt they wanted people who keep thinking for themselves and asking questions, continually examining what they know and do not know as they try to grow and move forward.

Ultimately, I think those criteria inevitably come down to stories of personal reflection and values, rather than the code itself.

### What makes good writing?

That raises the question of what makes a piece of writing good.

In my view, it is important to capture the process of reaching an insight approaching a truth through a personal event.

For example, what qualities might they expect from applicants under the keyword "challenge"?

Someone with the curiosity to continually explore the unknown, and the courage and will to forge ahead into a future full of uncertainty.

Values like these are akin to truths, and can be reached through some event in a person's life.

Whatever that event is, the journey toward insight is a story unique to the individual. I think writing sincerely and honestly about the emotions, pain, anguish, and realizations along the way may be a good way to show what kind of person "I" am.

# 1. Personal statement
![](/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-백엔드-최종-합격-후기-03.webp)

The Cohort 8 application had just one question, allowing up to 10,000 characters about experiences of challenge and immersion.

As it was the most important question in the selection process, I described periods that had profoundly changed my values.

I wrote about delving into philosophy to overcome physical pain and the mental suffering it brought, searching for meaning in the pain pervading my life, considering how I should accept my life, and the insights I gained from those times.

It was about 8,400 characters long.

<br>

I felt that reading more than a thousand personal statements would be enormously tiring for an evaluator.

Assuming most people would write about coding, I deliberately did not include much about it.

The first sentence of my personal statement was '**_Does life have meaning?_**'

I think I wrote a somewhat provocative essay because I wanted it to stand out among so many others, and hoped my sincerity would come through.

# 2. Precourse
![](/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-백엔드-최종-합격-후기-04.webp)

### Missions

The missions over the three weeks had already appeared in previous cohorts, so completing them was not difficult in itself.

Evaluating code from over a thousand people is not easy, and I did not think Woowa Tech Course ran the precourse to select those with outstanding skills.

In other words, **finishing it** was meaningful in itself; the differences in effort and individual ability within it did not seem to carry much weight in the evaluation.

Of course, I wanted this badly, and solving missions with other people was fun in itself, so I tried to introduce a new architecture each week and commit consistently.

Exchanging code reviews with various people was also a truly enjoyable and valuable experience.

### Reflection essays

We could write up to 5,000 characters of reflection for each week of the precourse.

I thought Woowa Tech Course valued _how do you think in a problem situation, and what do you feel while solving it?_ much more highly than the ability _can you solve the problem?_ itself.

So during the missions, I tried to record what I was thinking through and what insights those questions brought, rather than focusing solely on the missions, and weave those experiences into my reflections.

Each reflection was about 3,500–4,000 characters.

# 3. Open mission
![](/images/velog/우아한-테크코스-8기-백엔드-최종-합격-후기-05.webp)

### My concerns
Starting with Cohort 8, the Week 4 mission was removed and an open mission added.

Many people seemed to struggle with choosing a topic.

I also thought it over repeatedly without finding a suitable idea, and grew anxious as time kept passing.

I hoped my open mission topic could extend and bring closure to what I had felt during the precourse.

### What I felt during the precourse
One memorable realization from the precourse missions was that I needed to be wary of things I thought I knew.

During the precourse, I discovered that my belief that I understood and could use Java, architecture, separation of responsibilities, and so on had been an illusion.

More than whether I had the ability to do those things, it was somewhat shocking that I had not even recognized my own shortcomings.

So I decided my open mission would capture the process of finding something I mistakenly believed I knew well and understanding it fully.

### Topic

A friend suggested implementing Git in Java.

I thought I was reasonably proficient at using Git itself.

But thinking just a little deeper and asking, _Can I explain how Git works? _ immediately made me realize I knew nothing about it.

For the open mission, I implemented Git's behavior in Java and learned how Git performs version control.

Like the personal statement, the open mission reflection allowed up to 10,000 characters. I wrote about 7,400.
