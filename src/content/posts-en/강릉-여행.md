During the Level 3 break, I went to Gangneung for two days and one night with Luke and Moa.

I came back certain I had spent a wonderful time with people whose precious presence in my life would last.

We visited many places—the sea, the sunset, the night market—and every one was beautiful and lovely.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Something that stayed with me was Luke saying that seeing those things also made him feel sad.

I felt the same, actually.

Whenever we drove past the sea, I kept feeling as though I had to keep staring at it to take in the moment,

as though I would miss this time the moment I turned away.

After the trip, I would not know when I could see the sea again, and this was the only time I could see it with these people at this age.

At the same time, I did not like how those feelings seemed to diminish the lovely impressions I could enjoy right now.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

I think it is a bad habit of mine.

I learned something recently while reading a book.

Emotions are indescribable, and meaningful when we simply feel them and hold them in our hearts as they are,

but the moment we try to organize them into logical language that we can explain to others and ourselves, they become terribly simple and superficial.

Perhaps the way to treat romance and memories is to gather both the swelling emotion and the wistfulness rising on the other side into that one moment, and remember it in our hearts like a painting.

<p class="rilog-empty-block" aria-hidden="true"><br /></p>

Every moment together was joyful and meaningful.

I hope for more happiness with the people in my life.
