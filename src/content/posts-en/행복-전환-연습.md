I was struggling a lot and feeling depressed when I read this book.

I wanted to put my mistakes and guilt right, but because they belonged to the past, all I could do was try to justify them.

Yet that process only made the wounds inside me fester.

This book helped me in that situation.

### Never make that mistake again
I kept thinking about why I had acted that way and where things had gone wrong.

It seemed that if I could find the root of my mistake and understand it fully, I could put the past right.

But no matter how long I examined it, what had happened in the past did not change.

Instead, the more I thought, the more new reasons and assumptions emerged, requiring yet more thought to argue against them.

The thinking I began to escape pain kept holding me inside it.

The book says that struggling to get rid of uncomfortable emotions creates even greater suffering.

The depression and guilt the brain gives me are warnings telling me never to do that again; the more I ignore them and dig into my feelings, the more pain the brain tries to inflict.

When the brain encounters danger, it tries to prevent a repeat.

But even though I had heard the warning enough, I think I kept searching for the reason for my pain.

I need to remember that the harder I try to eliminate an emotion, the more central it becomes to my life.

### Time heals
In the end, all I could do was let it pass.

I cannot stop thoughts from appearing, but I can choose not to cling to them and keep searching for reasons.

Still, letting go does not mean pretending past mistakes never happened or justifying them as unavoidable.

I need to take past mistakes seriously while keeping them from tying me to suffering in the present.

I think that is the responsibility I can take for my past.

> You cannot stop the waves, but you can learn to surf.
