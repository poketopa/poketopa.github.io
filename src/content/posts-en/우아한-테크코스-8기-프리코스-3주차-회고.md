## Regrets from Week 2

After the Week 2 mission ended, I had some regrets.

Compared with Week 1, introducing MVC and a layered architecture was clearly a structural improvement.

But looking inside that structure, I could not shake the feeling that the truly important logic and data flow were still clumsy and fragile.

I was so busy applying a new design that I missed details in the code and failed to write proper tests.

Of course, I know it is impossible to integrate something perfectly on the first try, but I think I felt disappointed because the more I studied, the more flaws I saw in my code.

**For Week 3, I set a goal of strengthening the structure I had established in Week 2 and making the logic within it clearer.**

## Code review

Even after the Week 2 assignment ended, I asked several people for code reviews. Thankfully, some pointed out fundamental issues in my code.

The main feedback was as follows.

> **"Separate classes that perform behavior without holding state into utility classes."
"Do not include domain objects as DTO parameters."
"You are overusing private and static without clear criteria."
"Manage models as VOs so they validate themselves.**

Of all the advice, what struck me most was **"Manage models as VOs (Value Objects) so they validate themselves."**

Through Week 2, I had handled data as **primitive values (primitive types)** or simple collections and tried to validate those values in the Service layer.

But studying **VOs** and '**First-Class Collections**' made it clear what I had been misunderstanding.

The key was **making an object take responsibility for its own existence**.

> **If a value is wrapped in an object and that object validates itself when it is created, the very existence of the domain object already proves the integrity of the value.**

## Week 3 mission: Lotto

The Week 3 mission was 'Lotto,' the same as last year.

Unusually, this problem came with part of the Lotto class already written.

It also specified the following constraints for the Lotto class.

> **"Fields must remain private."
"No new fields other than numbers may be added."**

I had already solved the Lotto problem while running a study group to prepare for the precourse.

But at the time, I had not attached much significance to those constraints, treating them as mere handicaps.

This time, however, looking at the conditions again during the precourse, I realized they were clear guidance to **treat this object as a first-class collection and give it responsibility as a domain object**.

It was the moment I realized that the feedback from Weeks 1 and 2 aligned perfectly with the design direction of the Week 3 mission.

## Questions I considered during the mission

In Week 3, I created many domain objects and made them responsible for validation and logic.

It was my first experience with this, so naturally it involved many questions and choices.

> **The bonus number is just one number. Is creating a class solely for it really efficient?
When passing values around, should I use primitive values, or can I pass domain objects directly as parameters?**

Where Weeks 1 and 2 had been about **why** to use a technique, Week 3 required me to consider **who should hold responsibility** within that technique.

There was no obvious correct answer. So I weighed the advantages and disadvantages of each option, established my own criteria, and wrote code that let me explain to myself why each object should have its particular responsibility.

I thought that the very fact I was considering these questions was evidence of my growth.

## Finishing Week 3

Even as I submitted the Week 3 assignment, I wondered whether this design was the best it could be.

Applying a new design appropriately was genuinely difficult, and I still wish I might have written better code.

Even so, I am happy that I now made judgments and applied my own criteria to things I had not even known what to do with, or why I should do them, in Weeks 1 and 2.

The more I learn, the more keenly I feel my shortcomings, and the things I need to study feel like a mountain.

But I think even this unfamiliar fear and uncertainty are natural feelings I can experience because I am growing.
