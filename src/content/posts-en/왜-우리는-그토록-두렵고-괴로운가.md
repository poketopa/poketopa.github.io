I have always wanted to be in a good environment with good people.

Because I vaguely believed that would make me happy.

Woowacourse looked like that kind of place to me, and experiencing it firsthand, it truly was.

A place where I could have meaningful conversations with good people.

But even at Woowacourse, which I had wanted so badly, my heart was never entirely at ease.

Its “good environment” gave me vague anxiety and doubts about my potential,

and its “good people” turned what I had considered my strengths into ordinary qualities.

I realized that regardless of my circumstances, anxiety, depression, and impatience were eating away at me for one reason or another.

I talked with fellow participants about this a lot and confessed how I felt. Later, I learned that most of the people around me had similar concerns.

That experience surprised me quite a bit, because each person's worries, with their different reasons, were so unexpected.

A participant whose skills intimidated me felt impatient because others seemed to catch up so easily with what they had built,

while one who was warm to everyone trembled with anxiety, worrying that the others might dislike them.

It felt so strange that their worries began with the very strengths I saw in them, and I thought deeply about it.

Why do we fear others' judgments so much, doubt our own growth, and feel impatient about the situation itself?

Why am I so generous and optimistic toward others, yet harsh only toward myself?

Everyone will have different answers, but I wanted to look for mine in human instinct.

I thought acknowledging that humans are simply animals faithful to their instincts could help me forgive my contradictions and find a solution.

People like objectivity, intuition, and clear facts, and try to believe and follow only the thoughts reached through those ways of thinking.

But I think selfishness and emotion act first, in an unconscious realm deeper than objective thought.

People seem afraid to express what they believe is right, and afraid to criticize others' opinions even when they know those opinions are wrong.

Perhaps that is why people find it relatively easy to judge others by strict standards, yet find it so hard to assess themselves objectively.

I sincerely support and respect my fellow participants.

Each has their own strengths and appeal, and I believe in their potential and sincerely root for them.

The others see those qualities in me and support me, too.

With everyone believing in me and cheering me on, I was the only person unable to love myself.

Then is the self-disgust arising from my worry and anxiety objective?

I do not think it is at all.

I believe it is simply a byproduct of instinct.

Then there is no need for me to become consumed by it.

Of course, these thoughts will not make my worries disappear.

But recognizing that my feelings are not based on clear facts, and are simply a vague smokescreen produced by instinct, gives me tremendous strength to keep depression from consuming me.

Let me become someone who can care for myself as much as I can accept others.
